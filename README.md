# Barrier vs. Immune: Differential Expression Analysis of Lesional and Non-Lesional Skin in Atopic Dermatitis

## Overview

Atopic dermatitis (AD, aka eczema) affects about 1 in 10 people, and
there's actually an open debate in the field about what's driving it: is
it mainly an immune system problem, or does it start with something
breaking down in the skin's physical barrier? This project uses a public
RNA-seq dataset (Tsoi et al. 2019, *J Invest Dermatol*, GSE121212) with
paired lesional, non-lesional, and healthy skin samples to build a full
analysis pipeline, from the raw count data all the way to statistical
testing, pathway enrichment, and visualization, and see what the data
actually says about that immune-vs-barrier question, both in visibly
affected skin and in skin that looks normal but comes from someone with AD.

## Data

Source: [GSE121212](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE121212)
(Tsoi LC et al., *J Invest Dermatol* 2019, PMID 30641038).

The full dataset has 147 samples across three groups: atopic dermatitis
(AD), psoriasis (PSO), and healthy controls (CTRL). I used a smaller,
cleaner subset of that: 21 AD patients with paired lesional and
non-lesional samples, plus all 38 healthy controls (80 samples total).

Two groups in the raw file got excluded on purpose:
- **Psoriasis samples**, not relevant to what I'm asking here.
- **AD patients 032 to 037**. While digging into the metadata I found these
  actually belong to a separate study (Tsoi et al. 2020, *J Allergy Clin
  Immunol*, PMID 31891686) that used different clinical criteria for what
  counts as an "acute" vs. "chronic" lesion. Lumping them in with the main
  lesional group would mean mixing two different sampling protocols under
  the same label, so I left them out. The exact logic is in
  `scripts/curate_subset.py`.

The raw data file isn't tracked in this repo (see `data/raw/README.md` for
how to get it), and neither are the processed outputs. Everything gets
regenerated by running the pipeline.

## Setup

```bash
conda env create -f environment.yml
conda activate ad-rnaseq-analysis
```

Grab the raw data first. Instructions are in `data/raw/README.md`.

## Running the pipeline

Everything's wired up through Snakemake, so one command runs the whole
thing in the right order:

```bash
snakemake --cores 1
```

Or see the plan without actually running anything:
```bash
snakemake -n
```

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `curate_subset.py` | Parses the GEO column names, builds a clean metadata table, subsets to the AD/CTRL cohort described above |
| `qc_pca.py` | Checks sequencing depth, cleans up a couple of corrupted gene names, normalizes the data, runs PCA |
| `differential_expression.py` | Main comparison: AD lesional vs. healthy |
| `secondary_comparison.py` | Two more comparisons: non-lesional vs. healthy, and lesional vs. non-lesional (this one's paired by patient) |
| `enrichment_analysis.py` | Pathway enrichment against GO and KEGG, pulled live from Enrichr |
| `visualize_results.py` | Heatmap and boxplots for a curated set of genes |

Heads up: a full run takes about 20 minutes, mostly because DESeq2's model
fitting runs three separate times (once per comparison) and the enrichment
step is making live calls to Enrichr.

## What I found

### Differential expression
I ran three comparisons within the 80-sample cohort:

| Comparison | Model | Significant genes (padj<0.05, \|log2FC\|>1) |
|---|---|---|
| Lesional vs. healthy | Unpaired (`~condition`) | 3,208 / 31,360 |
| Non-lesional vs. healthy | Unpaired (`~condition`) | 85 / 31,360 |
| Lesional vs. non-lesional | Paired (`~patient_id + condition`) | 1,849 / 31,356 |

The top genes in the main comparison (lesional vs. healthy) turned out to
be cornified envelope/keratin genes, `LCE3D`, `LCE5A`, `KRT77`, ranked
above any single immune gene by p-value, which I didn't expect going in.
In the non-lesional vs. healthy comparison, 77 of the 85 significant genes
are *up*, not down, which tells you something: non-lesional AD skin isn't
just "a little noisy" compared to healthy skin, it's shifted in a
consistent direction.

### Enrichment
I ran pathway enrichment two ways: once against a small hand-picked set of
barrier vs. immune genes, and once against the full GO and KEGG databases.
They agree with each other. Immune/cytokine pathways come out as the
bigger, more extreme signal across the board (things like "Inflammatory
Response" and "Cytokine-Mediated Signaling Pathway," with NES over 2.4 and
FDR basically zero in every comparison). But barrier and keratinocyte
differentiation pathways are also genuinely, independently significant,
just smaller in effect and narrower in scope (e.g. "Keratinocyte
Differentiation," NES 2.04, FDR 0.0004). One thing I found interesting:
the immune signal shows up just as strongly in non-lesional skin as in
lesional skin, which suggests the immune activation isn't just a side
effect of visible lesions forming. It seems to be there already.

### Visualization
When I clustered a curated panel of 23 genes (top DE hits plus known
barrier/immune markers), it split cleanly into two groups moving in
opposite directions across the three conditions, and non-lesional samples
consistently landed in between healthy and lesional across most of the
panel. Same story as the PCA and DE results, just visible at the level of
individual genes now.

## Limitations

Being upfront about a few things I ran into:

- **Results aren't perfectly identical across machines.** The paired
  comparison (21 patient-blocking terms on only 42 samples) showed some
  floating-point instability depending on hardware. I actually tracked
  this down and confirmed it's a BLAS-library difference, not a bug. I
  checked how much it mattered by directly correlating the results from
  two different machines: 0.976 correlation on fold-change, 0.92+ on
  significance ranking across roughly 29,500 genes. So the core result
  holds up, but a handful of genes sitting right at the significance
  cutoff can flip depending on the machine.
- **One patient (AD_023) is an outlier**, flagged by both PCA and Cook's
  distance. I checked what happens if I drop them: the main comparison
  barely moves (under 2% change), but the two secondary comparisons are
  more sensitive. The non-lesional vs. healthy gene count shifts by about
  40%, and the paired comparison's top 10 genes only overlap 6/10. That's
  more about those comparisons having less statistical power to begin
  with than about this one patient being bad data. I kept them in for
  consistency across all three comparisons.
- **GO terms overlap a lot.** A lot of the "960 significant terms" in one
  comparison are really the same underlying genes showing up under
  slightly different pathway names, so that number shouldn't be read as
  960 independent discoveries.
- **Gene symbol matching isn't perfect.** Enrichment relies on exact gene
  symbol matches between my data and Enrichr's gene sets, so a few genes
  probably get missed due to naming differences. This is a general
  limitation of symbol-based enrichment, not something specific to this
  analysis.

## Why this dataset

I have eczema myself, and wanted to actually understand what's happening
at the level the data shows it, not just the surface-level explanation you
usually get from patient-facing health sites.
